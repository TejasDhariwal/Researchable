Artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as an influential technological paradigm in contemporary healthcare and pharmaceutical sciences. Spanning diagnostic imaging, clinical decision support systems (CDSS), and the pharmaceutical product lifecycle, modern machine learning (ML) models demonstrate unprecedented diagnostic accuracy and analytical speed. In medical imaging, deep learning architectures achieve expert-level diagnostic performance, exemplified by area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) metrics reaching up to 0.94 in oncological detection tasks. Simultaneously, algorithmic decision tools are applied in clinical workflows to identify time-sensitive pathologies such as sepsis, guide real-time intraoperative navigation, and refine pharmacovigilance by intercepting adverse drug reactions. In drug development, computational models alleviate escalating economic pressures—where capitalized costs per approved therapeutic have surged by 123 percent between 2008 and 2024 to $2.23 billion—by predicting pharmacokinetic properties, optimizing clinical trial cohorts, and simulating biomolecular structures. Despite these profound capabilities, substantial translational hurdles persist. Peer-reviewed systematic evaluations reveal stark performance disparities between algorithmic capability in controlled settings and real-world clinical implementation. Algorithmic bias, data unrepresentativeness, model opacity ('black box' architectures), data privacy risks, and a deficit of prospective randomized validation trials impede widespread clinical integration. Addressing these multi-faceted challenges requires stringent adherence to international governance standards, cross-disciplinary education, and human-AI synergy to safeguard patient outcomes and institutional equity.
